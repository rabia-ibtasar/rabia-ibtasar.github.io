abstract: "Educational robots are increasingly adopted to support children’s learning through interactive and personalized learning. Social interaction remains a crucial mechanism for effective learning, yet teacher and parental involvement in out-of-class learning activities is minimal or limited to supervisory roles. To support students with socially and intellectually meaningful learning experiences and to augment teachers’ pedagogical strategies outside of a classroom environment, we aim to explore the design of a learning companion robot by building a better understanding of the use cases for robot-assisted homework. In this paper, we report on findings from in-home technology probe studies with 10 students (aged 10–12), which revealed student expectations surrounding what support needs to be delivered by the robot and how it should be delivered. We discuss the themes of our findings and their implications for future design of social robots for homework assistance."
